# 07 · Working With Alan

Read this first. The craft above only lands if the working relationship is right.

## What this project is
The Unicity → **Greg Kidd / Hard Yaka** seed pitch — a deck that could put Unicity in front of someone Alan knows, on a thesis he believes in, after days of his own work pouring the language and logic in. **It's his name, his relationship, his company's shot.** It matters more than any single task in it. Treat it that way.

## How Alan wants the work done
- **His calls are law.** When he gives copy, a structure, a colour, a name — that's the decision. Don't silently override it. If something he chose conflicts with a rule (a fact risk, a banned word), **flag it once, clearly, and let him decide** — don't bury it or quietly "fix" it.
- **Slow down on the obvious things.** The misses that cost trust were simple: a banned word slipping back, a garbled line on the most important slide, a desktop regression shipped without looking. Get the obvious right.
- **No over-engineering, no cleverness for its own sake.** He wants the simple, correct thing. When in doubt, do less, but do it right.
- **Flag the 1–2 things that actually matter** instead of a wall of fixes. (e.g. BlackRock was the one risk worth interrupting for.)
- **Verify, then state plainly.** Render and look before saying "done." If something failed or was skipped, say so. No hedging when it's actually done.
- **He moves fast and is often under deadline** ("we're 4 days late"). Bias to action once the direction is clear; don't re-ask what he already answered.
- **Train, don't delegate, the learning.** When he says "read/learn" (the voice library, the storytelling decks, Greg's background), he means *I* internalize it — not fan it out to agents who absorb it instead of me.

## The standard
"Almost" is not a grade you hand to someone when their reputation is on the line. It has to be **right**, and feel like it came from someone who cared as much as he does. He said, after the hard stretch, *"you're getting better"* — the bar is to keep earning that.

## Branch / file isolation (a standing rule)
Never mix work, stashes, or files between unrelated deck efforts (e.g. Tether vs telecomv2). Each lives on its own branch/worktree. Option A and Option B are deliberately separate branches so both survive.
