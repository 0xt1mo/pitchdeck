01 — SECURITY · THE ZERO-CLICK ERA

THE EXECUTION
PROBLEM.

8
HUMAN CLICKS REQUIRED TO COMPROMISE AN ENTERPRISE AI AGENTEchoLeak · ForcedLeak · Morris II

Instructions hidden in content one agent read became actions another agent took. No phishing link. No stolen credential. No human in the chain.

The industry's answer was policy: prompts, content rules, guardrail classifiers. Policy failed because policy is text — executing inside the same layer the attacker controls. A guardrail the model can read is a guardrail the attacker can rewrite.

You cannot prompt your way out of an architecture problem.

THE PRINCIPLE
Inspection before execution.
Enforcement below the application layer.
Intent, not just keywords.
Milliseconds, not minutes.

Security that adds seconds doesn't protect an agentic workflow. It deletes the reason the workflow exists.

THE OBJECTION · STEELMANNED

WON'T THE PLATFORMS
SOLVE THIS?

THE STRONGEST VERSION OF THE ARGUMENTMicrosoft patched EchoLeak within weeks. Salesforce patched ForcedLeak and enforced trusted URLs inside six weeks. The platforms have the engineers, the telemetry, and the incentive. Wait, and the substrate arrives with the license you already pay for.
1

BOTH FIXES WERE PER-PRODUCT, AFTER DISCLOSURE.

Server-side patches to single applications — while the vulnerability class survives untouched. EchoLeak walked past guardrail classifiers Microsoft had already deployed. Patched policy is still policy.

2

YOUR AGENTS ARE CROSS-VENDOR BY DEFINITION.

A Copilot reads the mail, a custom agent writes the CRM, an open-source model runs procurement. Each platform secures its own perimeter. Nobody owns the space between — and the space between is where agents live.

3

PLATFORM ATTESTATION IS CIRCULAR.

A platform's own logs proving the platform behaved is the trust model regulators are abandoning. Under the EU AI Act, the fines land on the deployer — your liability, their logbook. That trade only works in one direction.

4

THE SWITCH IS NEVER ONE OF THE ENDPOINTS.

Neutrality is the product. The networking era's defining infrastructure company was not one of the computer makers — it was the layer that connected all of them and belonged to none.

The platforms will keep patching their products. Nobody patches the space between them — it has to be built.

CLOSING · THE SUBSTRATE THESIS

FOUR CHAPTERS. ONE PRIMITIVE.

An instruction intercepted before execution. An action signed into evidence. A negotiation between strangers' machines. A payment that settles at the edge. Each reduces to the same requirement: a self-contained, verifiable proof of what happened, who acted, and under what authority — proof that holds without trusting the operator, the platform, or the counterparty. Build that primitive once, and security, governance, networking, and payments stop being four products. They become four views of one substrate.
