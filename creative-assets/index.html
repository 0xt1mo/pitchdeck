01 — SECURITY · THE ZERO-CLICK ERA

THE EXECUTION PROBLEM.

8
HUMAN CLICKS REQUIRED TO COMPROMISE AN ENTERPRISE AI AGENTEchoLeak · ForcedLeak · Morris II

Instructions hidden in content one agent read became actions another agent took. No phishing link. No stolen credential. No human in the chain.

The industry's answer was policy: prompts, content rules, guardrail classifiers. Policy failed because policy is text — executing inside the same layer the attacker controls. A guardrail the model can read is a guardrail the attacker can rewrite.

The blocker isn't capability — it's trust. 64% of enterprises name data-security unpreparedness, not the model, as why agentic AI stalls (Morgan Stanley, via Securiti 2026). You cannot prompt your way out of an architecture problem.

THE PRINCIPLE
Inspection before execution.
Enforcement below the application layer.
Intent, not just keywords.
Data-flow, not model behavior.
Milliseconds, not minutes.

AI security is data-flow security — most of the OWASP Top 10 for LLMs are data vulnerabilities; the check must land at retrieval, not in an after-the-fact audit (Securiti, 2026). Security that adds seconds doesn't protect an agentic workflow. It deletes the reason the workflow exists.

03 — NETWORKING · A2A · MCP · CROSS-ENTERPRISE

THE INTEROPERABILITY PROBLEM.

0%
GROWTH IN MULTI-AGENT WORKFLOWS IN FOUR MONTHSDatabricks · Jun–Oct 2025

Coordination stops at the firewall, and the value doesn't. The endgame is your procurement agent negotiating with a supplier's pricing agent — between organizations that share no platform, no ledger, and no reason to trust each other.

No enterprise can pick winners at this velocity — so the durable position was never a protocol. It is the switch: the protocol-agnostic layer every connection passes through, whatever language it speaks. Whoever builds the switching layer for agents builds one of the defining infrastructure companies of the decade.

The mesh is already irreducibly heterogeneous — 78% of companies run two or more model families, 59% run three or more, and multi-agent orchestration is the #1 use case (Databricks, 2026). No single protocol can win by adoption. The switch is forced by the math, not chosen.

THE PRINCIPLE
Protocol-agnostic.
Identity-mandatory.
Cross-enterprise by design.
Every machine conversation on the record.

A conversation between machines that move money must be as auditable as a wire between banks.

Server-side patches to single applications — while the vulnerability class survives untouched. EchoLeak walked past guardrail classifiers Microsoft had already deployed. Patched policy is still policy — and the platforms admit it: Red Hat ships agent orchestration while listing the permission-and-trust layer as a roadmap item, not a shipped one.

2

YOUR AGENTS ARE CROSS-VENDOR BY DEFINITION.

78% of companies run two or more LLM model families; 59% run three or more, and multi-agent orchestration is now the #1 use case — Databricks. A Copilot reads the mail, a custom agent writes the CRM, an open-source model runs procurement. Each platform secures its own perimeter. Nobody owns the space between — and the space between is where agents live.

3

PLATFORM ATTESTATION IS CIRCULAR.

A platform's own logs proving the platform behaved is the trust model regulators are abandoning. Under the EU AI Act, the fines land on the deployer — your liability, their logbook. That trade only works in one direction.

4

THE SWITCH IS NEVER ONE OF THE ENDPOINTS.

Neutrality is the product. The networking era's defining infrastructure company was not one of the computer makers — it was the layer that connected all of them and belonged to none.

The platforms will keep patching their products. Nobody patches the space between them — it has to be built.
